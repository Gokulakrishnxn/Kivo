What you get

Small app. Room for your whole day.

Kivo has a deliberately small surface. It is a key you press, not an assistant you talk to.

One global key

Works in every app, including full-screen. Push to talk, or double-tap to latch it on. Rebind it to anything, including a bare modifier.

Cleans as it goes

Punctuation, capitalisation, fillers, stutters and your own vocabulary — handled before the text ever reaches your cursor.

Lands at the cursor

Straight into Slack, VS Code, Word or your browser as a single undoable edit. If an app refuses, Kivo pastes and puts your clipboard back.

Stays on your machine

Audio is never uploaded and never written to disk. No telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting, nothing to sign up for.

Built for your Mac

A universal app for Apple Silicon and Intel. This early preview is English-only; Windows support is planned.

Yours to read

MIT licensed and public. For a tool holding a microphone permission, being readable is the only way the promises above mean anything.

How it works

Hold. Speak. Carry on.

1

Hold your key

The microphone opens on key-down and closes on key-up, so the indicator in your menu bar is telling you the truth.

2

Say the thing

NVIDIA's Parakeet model runs on your own hardware and returns words, punctuation, capitalisation and timings.

3

Let go

Cleanup runs, and the finished sentence lands at your cursor as one edit. One undo removes it if you change your mind.

Straight answers

The questions people actually ask.

Is it really free?

Yes. MIT licensed, no account, no subscription, no paid tier holding features back. The name and logo stay with the author; the code is yours.

Why does it need accessibility access?

That permission is how text reaches your cursor and how the global key is seen. Kivo reads nothing from your screen — and you can verify that in the source.

Does it work offline?

Entirely. The model downloads once, verified against a pinned hash. After that you can stay offline forever and nothing changes.

Is it finished?

No — 0.1.0 is a pre-release. It works, but it is unsigned, slower than its own target, English-only and macOS-only. The release notes list every known limitation rather than leaving you to find them.

Before you install the macOS preview

Version 0.1.0 supports macOS 11+, Apple Silicon and Intel. It is English-only, unsigned, and slower than the project’s latency target. Windows is planned.

macOS may block the first launch. Open Kivo once, dismiss the warning, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. Download the recognition model during setup; dictation then runs offline.
