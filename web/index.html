The whole product

Clean, not just transcribed.

Every dictation tool can turn sound into words. The reason none of them replaced your keyboard is what comes back: an unpunctuated wall of text with every “um” faithfully preserved, and a self-correction you now have to untangle by hand.

Kivo removes fillers, collapses stutters, resolves the sentence you started over, and applies your own vocabulary — before the text ever reaches your cursor. The first output is meant to be the final one.

And when you want exactly what you said, Raw mode gives you that with nothing touched.

cleanup · tiers
  • Fillers and stutters — “um”, “uh”, “the uh the” removed by deterministic rules in under a millisecond.
  • Punctuation and capitalisation — restored by the speech model itself, so it costs no extra time.
  • Your vocabulary — names, acronyms and jargon that generic models always get wrong.
  • Modes — standard, message, email, notes, code. A mode changes only the cleanup, never the recognition.
  • Optional local polish — a small on-device model repairs self-corrections and register. Off by default, because it costs time.
Everywhere you type

One key. Any application.

Kivo is a key you press, not an assistant you talk to. No wake word, no conversation, no window to copy out of. It lives in your menu bar and stays silent until you hold the key.

Text goes in through the accessibility layer as a single edit, so one undo removes it. Where an app refuses synthetic input — terminals and Electron apps often do — Kivo pastes instead and puts your clipboard back exactly as it found it.

On-device

Your voice never leaves your machine.

Client files, medical notes, legal drafts, unreleased code. Plenty of people are not permitted to stream their microphone to someone else's server, which is exactly why they still type.

Kivo runs NVIDIA's Parakeet speech model locally. Audio lives in a buffer that is zeroed after use and is never written to disk. There is no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting, and nothing to sign up for.

The microphone opens when you press the key and closes when you let go — so the indicator in your menu bar tells you the truth.

How it works

Five stages, one process, no network.

01 · CAPTURE

Hold the key

The microphone opens into a lock-free ring buffer, and closes the moment you release.

02 · SEGMENT

Trim the silence

Resampled to 16 kHz, then voice detection cuts the dead air you always leave at each end.

03 · RECOGNISE

Parakeet TDT

NVIDIA's model returns words, punctuation, capitalisation and timings — on your GPU or CPU.

04 · CLEAN

Make it usable

Fillers out, stutters collapsed, your vocabulary applied, formatted for the mode you chose.

05 · DELIVER

At your cursor

Inserted as one undoable edit, with a clipboard fallback that restores what was there.

Open source

Read the source. Fork it. Ship it.

Kivo is MIT licensed. Not source-available, not open-core, not free-until-we-raise. Clone it, change it, sell it — the licence genuinely permits all of that.

For a tool that holds a microphone permission and injects keystrokes, being readable is not a marketing position. It is the only way the privacy claims above can be more than a promise.

The Kivo name and logo stay with the author, so a fork gets its own identity. Everything else is yours.

Contributing guide Product requirements
stack
  • SHELLTauri v2 — a Rust core with a ~10 MB binary, resident all day under 150 MB.
  • SPEECHNVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B via ONNX Runtime — CoreML, CUDA, DirectML or CPU.
  • AUDIOcpal capture, rubato resampling, Silero voice detection.
  • INTERFACEReact, TypeScript, Vite, and the design tokens this page is built from.
  • STORAGESQLite, encrypted at rest, key held in the OS keychain. Or nothing at all.
